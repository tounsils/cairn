# Research findings

Gathered 2026-09-14. **Read the source-quality note first, because it changes how much of this you should lean on.**

## Source quality, stated up front

Reddit blocks direct fetching and X/Facebook were not reachable, so this is search plus primary sources rather than community mining.

**Trust these two.** They are first-party and specific:
- Puzzmo's pricing and what sits behind its paywall (Hearst press material and a Game Developer interview with Zach Gage)
- Duolingo's Friend Streak retention figure (Duolingo's own engineering blog)

**Treat these as directional only.** A large share of the category commentary comes from daily-game aggregator sites (`earthguessr.com`, `dinogame.gg`, `playlin.io`, `alldle.net`) which exist to drive traffic to daily games and have an obvious incentive to describe the genre as booming. Their traffic and growth claims are not audited. This is the same failure mode as the escape-room market reports, where three sources disagreed by 10x.

---

## 1. The daily format is genuinely proven

- NYT games have grown from roughly 15% of time spent in the bundle to **over 50%, overtaking news**.
- In Q1 2025 the Times added 250,000 digital-only subscribers, **110,000 of them from Games**.
- NYT annual subscription revenue passed $1B for the first time, with games credited as a driver.

So the format is not the risk. The crew layer is.

## 2. Ads cannot pay for this

Web game ad revenue benchmarks land around **$10 to $40 per thousand DAU per month**. A game with 20,000 daily actives therefore earns roughly **$400 a month**.

Stated plainly: the free-to-play-with-hints model that started this conversation has a ceiling of a few hundred dollars a month at a scale most daily games never reach. This single number is why the design moved to subscriptions.

Realistic band quoted for a first web game overall: **$500 to $3,000 a month**.

## 3. Puzzmo is the comparable worth studying

- Built by **two people over three years**. Zach Gage and Orta Therox.
- Acquired by **Hearst** in December 2023, rolled out across 50+ brands.
- **Puzzmo Plus: $3.99/month or $39.99/year.** Daily puzzles stay free.
- Behind the paywall: **archive, leaderboards, and groups.**
- Gage's stated goal: *"to build the world's best puzzle website that is run as a very successful and very profitable business forever"*, explicitly contrasted with venture-backed exponential growth.
- Growth came from a **key-sharing scarcity mechanic**: 104,000 people made 932,000 attempts to get in, at **zero ad spend**.

Three things to take from this. Groups are already a proven paywall item, which is direct evidence for the crew model. The price anchor is $39.99/year. And a profitable-forever puzzle business is an achievable shape, not a fantasy.

## 4. Shared streaks work, with a number attached

Duolingo's **Friend Streaks** let up to five people share a streak. Users with at least one Friend Streak are **22% more likely to complete their daily lesson**, described as the strongest social retention layer they have built.

Their stated psychology is exactly the mechanic to copy: *missing a day does not just break your number, it lets your partner down.*

The design consequence, which Duolingo's five-person cap hints at: keep crews small enough that each person feels individually needed.

## 5. The gap, stated precisely

Searching specifically for asynchronous daily puzzles with a **shared group outcome** turns up nothing. What exists is adjacent but different:

| What exists | Why it is not this |
|---|---|
| Jigsaw co-op (Puzzle Together, Foony, PuzzlesO) | Real-time, relaxing, no daily hook, no streak |
| Codenames Online | Synchronous, session-based, not daily |
| Crosswords With Friends | Async but **competitive**: compare completion times |
| Worldle, Globle, Travle | Solo play, sharing happens outside the product |

The pattern in the literature on async multiplayer puzzles is that "async" means *compare results afterwards*. That is competition with a delay, not cooperation.

**And the growth research says the social layer is already the engine.** On Travle specifically: the sharing feature is described as the underrated growth driver, because in group chats one player's daily post nudges the others to play and then to share, turning it into an ambient daily ritual. That behaviour is happening in WhatsApp. No product owns it.

## 6. The geography niche is active and competitive

Named as the fastest-growing mapping games of 2026: **EarthGuessr, Globle, Worldle, Travle, City Guesser**. Travle is described as the breakout, with daily actives roughly tripling across 2026 (aggregator claim, unaudited).

Read this as demand validation rather than an empty niche. It also means incumbents are awake: Teuteuf Games make both Worldle and Travle and could add crews quickly.

## 7. The post-Wordle graveyard

Hundreds of daily puzzle games launched in the 18 months after Wordle. **Most did not survive a year.** The survivors named (Quordle, Connections, Crosswordle) differentiated rather than cloned.
