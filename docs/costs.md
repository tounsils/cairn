# What the MVP costs to run

```

```

---

## The headline

**Infrastructure is not your cost problem and will not become one.** At the target scale in [`concept.md`](concept.md) — 1,000 paying crews, about 4,000 people, $2,499/month of revenue — the whole stack runs for **about $7 a month**, which is a quarter of one percent of revenue.

Two things dominate instead, and neither is a server.

## 1. Stripe costs 14x more than all infrastructure combined

| | Launch (100 crews) | Target (1,000) | Success (10,000) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $250 | $2,499 | $24,992 |
| Infrastructure | $6 | **$7** | $13 |
| Stripe fees | $10 | **$97** | $975 |
| Combined as % of revenue | 6.3% | **4.2%** | 4.0% |

Payment processing at 2.9% + $0.30 is the floor on this business, and it barely moves with volume. Infrastructure, by contrast, gets proportionally *cheaper* as you grow: 2.4% of revenue at launch, 0.05% at success.

The practical consequence: **do not spend engineering time optimising hosting.** A day spent shaving the infra bill saves single-digit dollars. The $0.30 fixed fee per transaction is the thing worth designing around, which argues for annual billing over monthly — twelve $2.50 charges cost $3.60 in fixed fees alone, against $0.30 once.

## 2. The map decision swings the answer by a factor of a hundred

Same product, same users, four different choices about imagery:

| Map approach | Launch | Target | Success |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Static geometry (country boundaries as an asset) | **$0** | **$0** | **$0** |
| Self-hosted vector tiles, PMTiles on Cloudflare R2 | $0.07 | $0.07 | $9 |
| Hosted tile API (Mapbox, per map load) | $0 | **$350** | $5,750 |
| Street-level imagery (Google Street View Static) | $42 | **$690** | $7,170 |

At the target tier that is **$0 versus $690 for the same number of players.** Street View alone would consume 28% of revenue, and it scales linearly with success rather than amortising.

### The design already avoids this, and that was not an accident

The genre recommended in [`concept.md`](concept.md) — country and place guessing, in the Worldle / Globle / Travle family — **does not stream a basemap at all.** Those games render simplified country boundaries shipped once as a static asset and cached by the browser. There is no tile server, no per-load meter, and no vendor.

Street-level imagery is the expensive genre *and* the one with licensing exposure. Avoiding it was already the right call for content-pipeline reasons; it turns out to be the single biggest cost decision in the product as well.

**If you later want a real basemap**, PMTiles on R2 is the answer, not a hosted tile API. R2 charges **no egress fees at all**, which is the entire game when you are shipping map bytes. A hosted API bills per map load whether or not anything changed.

## 3. Provider choice barely matters, except in two places

For a workload this small — one session per person per day, a few hundred bytes per guess, no video and no inference — **AWS, GCP and Cloudflare are all effectively free.** Lambda's 1M requests/month never expires; Cloud Run gives 2M; Workers gives 100k/day free or 10M for $5. DynamoDB, Firestore and D1 all absorb this volume inside their free tiers.

So do not choose a provider on compute price. Choose on the two things that actually differ:

**Egress.** Cloudflare R2 is $0/GB. AWS is $0.09/GB after 100GB. GCP is roughly $0.12/GB. For anything tile-shaped this is decisive, and it is why the recommendation is Cloudflare for static assets even if the API lives elsewhere.

**Email.** AWS SES is **$0.10 per 1,000**. Resend, Postmark and SendGrid are roughly 10x that at volume. If this ever sends a daily nudge to 4,000 people — 120,000 messages a month — SES costs $12 and Resend costs about $110.

The honest answer is that the cheapest sane stack is **not a single provider**: Cloudflare for the site, assets and API, AWS SES for mail, Stripe for money.

### Design your way out of the email bill

Better still, do not send a daily email. The entire thesis of this product is that the crew already lives in a group chat, and **that group chat is the notification channel.** One person posting their result nudges everyone else; that is exactly the growth loop the research found driving Travle and Worldle. Email should carry invites and maybe a weekly digest, which is a couple of dollars a month forever.

| Layer | Choice | Target-tier cost |
|---|---|---:|
| Site + API | Cloudflare Workers / Pages | $5 |
| Map | Static country geometry, no tile server | $0 |
| Database | Cloudflare D1 (SQLite) | $0 |
| Email | AWS SES, invites and digests only | $0.60 |
| Domain | `.com` | ~$1 |
| **Infrastructure total** | | **~$7/month** |
| Stripe | 2.9% + $0.30, billed annually | $97 |

## What would actually change this

The model is only as good as its assumptions, and these are the ones worth arguing with:

- **Street-level imagery.** Adds ~$690/month at target and scales with users. This is the one decision that could make the product structurally unprofitable.
- **A daily email to every member.** Turns $0.60 into $12 on SES, or $110 on a developer-friendly sender.
- **Monthly rather than annual billing.** Twelve $0.30 fixed fees per crew per year instead of one.
- **Anything with per-seat vendor pricing.** At 40,000 users, a $0.10/user/month tool costs more than the entire rest of the stack.

Everything else in the model — session counts, tiles per session, API calls, database writes — can be wrong by a factor of five and the total moves by single-digit dollars. That insensitivity is itself the finding.
